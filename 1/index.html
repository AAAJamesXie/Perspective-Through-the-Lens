The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

A / The comparison

Close up / Step back

The framing stays similar. The proportions do not.

↗ Compare the nose, ears, and outline of the face. In the close image, features nearest the camera are exaggerated. With the camera farther away, the relative distances to those features become more similar.

B / The sequence

A gradual change

Five frames, from near to far.

Face-to-lens distances and on-screen zoom settings are the photographer's notes. The 35 mm equivalent focal lengths (14, 20, 28, 48, and 99 mm) are the exact values recorded in the original HEIC metadata.

From 10 cm to 100 cm, the camera moves back while the field of view narrows. The face stays roughly the same size, but its proportions and the visible background change.

C / Why the angle changes

It was where
the camera stood.

At 10 cm, the nose is noticeably closer to the lens than the ears. That difference is large relative to the camera-to-face distance, so near features look exaggerated. At 100 cm, the same depth difference is a much smaller fraction of the total distance, making the face appear flatter and more balanced.

The first image's 14 mm equivalent lens captures a wide view; the last image's 99 mm equivalent framing shows a much narrower one. Zooming in as the camera moves back keeps the face roughly the same size. If the camera stayed in one place, zoom alone would change the crop, not the facial perspective.

THE TAKEAWAYPerspective follows position.
Zoom follows framing.
